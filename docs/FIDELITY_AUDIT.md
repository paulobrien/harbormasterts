# Harbor Master HD fidelity audit

## Scope and method

This document tracks what can be established from the supplied **Harbor Master HD 2.0.1** iPad IPA and what remains uncertain. The IPA contains an ARMv7 32-bit Mach-O executable, not the original source code. Its symbol and debug tables retain useful names, paths and addresses. We inventoried these and inspected individual functions using ARM disassembly and compared their observable effects with the TypeScript implementation, the supplied level data, and [recorded iPad gameplay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BgPDMZNsuzk). Original assets and executable are user-supplied and are not included in this repository.

The executable contains 27,240 symbol/debug records and references 159 distinct compilation-unit paths under the original project. These include third-party advertising, purchase, social, network, archive, rendering and audio libraries, in addition to the game. A symbol record is not a source line, function, or independently verified behavior. The inventory is broad; it is **not an exhaustive function-by-function decompilation** or a proof that every behavior is understood. Equivalent-looking math and game feel still require side-by-side comparison with a running original.

The supplied HD IPA includes the Treasure Island map and its assets. Its in-app purchase UI and other map-specific game classes are present, but the other HD map files themselves are not in this IPA. Their behavior cannot be fully exercised or reproduced from this copy alone. Online services, multiplayer, advertisements, purchases, leaderboards and the original operating system integrations were not recreated or tested as part of this single-player map audit.

## What we inspected

- `cBoat` constructor, path, motion and simulation; `cGameController` input, pacing, spawning, collisions, auto-docking, scoring and game-state methods; `cDock` and level data.
- `cLand` and `cRock` distinctions, dock and land coordinate systems, and the supplied level's collision polygons, pacing records and dock settings.
- User-interface and localization resources, audio files and named methods for preferences, records, high scores, saving and loading.
- Existing browser gameplay, importer, persistence, controls and regression tests.

The current implementation is an independent recreation. It loads the map, graphics and audio from a separately staged game-data bundle, or can import them from an IPA selected by the player if needed. A playable deployment serves its staged bundle to browsers; scores and saved shifts remain on the device. The repository does not include original assets. The app is not running or translating the original executable.

## Evidence and coverage

| Area | Original evidence | Current status / confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Map geometry | `hmhd0001.lvl` specifies a 1024 × 768 map, six docks and three land polygons. Land coordinates are centered with Y upwards; dock Y uses the bottom-left origin. | The importer reads the player's original level. Regression checks exercise the map's dock approaches and departures. Exact rendering and collision geometry remain subject to visual/gameplay comparison. |
| Boat sizes and motion | `cBoat` constructor (`0x5134`) configures maximum speeds of 40, 20 and 10 game units/s for small, medium and large boats. `handleTurn` (`0x3470`) has distinct angular limits. During path following, `cBoat::simulate` (`0x638c`) advances position along path segments using a speed × time budget, while rotating the visible orientation separately. `spawnBoat` (`0xd4d8`) assigns capacities of 1, 2 and 4 cargo items. | The recreation uses these speeds, turn limits and capacities, traverses drawn path segments by distance and uses the verified sprite regions. Exact turning and collision outlines remain subject to comparison against a running original. |
| Cargo and hull sprites | `cCargo` (`0x7c48`) uses 16 × 16 atlas sprites for each cargo colour. `cBoat::addCargo` (`0x3974`) places them at size-specific offsets; simulation removes matching items from the front without repositioning those left on board. Treasure Island begins with `allowMultiCargoBoats 0`, but its score-16 pacing stage enables mixed cargo when that stage is applied. A [large mixed-cargo boat is visible at 5:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BgPDMZNsuzk&t=306s). `updateTextureCoords` (`0x3208`) selects red or grey hull frames based on whether the route ends at an appropriate destination. | Cargo and hulls use the original atlas when supplied. Mixed boats unload matching cargo at each dock and retain the other colours and their positions until routed to another dock. Cargo slots and the active pacing stage survive saved shifts, including older saves. Route validity and docking edge cases may still differ from the original. |
| Collision warnings | `cGameController::draw` (`0x13424`) draws one of three red atlas outlines under a boat with a warning. The sprites are 37, 50 and 75 units wide, with distinct alignment offsets for medium and large boats. | All three outlines are drawn beneath warning boats, alongside their individual warning horns. Proximity detection is still an approximation and needs comparison with original gameplay. |
| Routes | `cBoat::handleTouchMoved` starts a path after a movement of more than 25 units, then samples points above 9 units and invokes its curve routine for gaps of at least 100 units. `cBoat::addPathPointsUsingCurve` (`0x3b70`) invokes an eight-sample curve helper named `cBSpline3D::InBetween` (`0x4bf84`); the spline constructor uses half the endpoint distance for each Bézier handle. `cGameController::handleTouchMoved` (`0xebb0`) checks drawn segments against land. | Simultaneous touch, initial and subsequent sampling thresholds, cubic interpolation and shoreline clipping are implemented. Dock-snap curves are also clipped against land, including when the matching berth is on the far side of an island. Touch responsiveness and the reconstructed path representation still need side-by-side validation. |
| Route and wake appearance | `cBoat::drawPath` (`0x314c`) draws a continuous line: translucent blue at width 4 when the path ends at a dock or map edge, otherwise opaque red at width 3. The separate `cBoatWake` uses a 29 × 22 foam sprite from the HD sprite atlas, with up to 20 segments per boat. Small, medium and large boats emit wake at 0.15, 0.3 and 0.5-second intervals; segments fade and shrink over size-dependent durations. | Continuous routes and a separate fading wake use those colours, intervals and the sprite from the player's own files. Wake placement is reconstructed from the boat's movement; saved shifts regenerate the visual wake after loading. Exact appearance and timing still need side-by-side verification against a running original. |
| Dock feedback glow | `cGameController::handleTouchMoved` (`0xebb0`) makes a soft white 32 × 32 atlas sprite visible at a matching, unblocked dock when a drawn route reaches it. It starts at three times its sprite size and 0.75 opacity; `simulate` decreases both scale and opacity by 2 per second. The same sprite also flashes at the beginning of a touch. | Touches and accepted dock targets now show the same brief, fading sprite from the player's own files. Exact appearance still needs comparison with a running original. |
| Land and rocks | `landElement` corresponds to `cLand` and `rockElement` to a separate `cRock`. The boat simulation reflects boats from land and clears the route; rock collision can end the shift. Treasure Island has no `rockElement`. | Shoreline reflection and route clearing are implemented for each traveled segment, including docking and previously saved paths. Auto-docking checks for land between the boat and berth. Collision uses the boat's center against the imported land polygons; exact native collision and visual hull overlap remain under review. There is no test of original rock hazards on other levels. |
| Screen edge | Native boat simulation reflects loaded boats back from a screen edge, allows unloaded boats to leave, and treats new arrivals separately. | Implemented and tested for all four edges, but exact spawn-entry and reflection details are approximate. |
| Docks and score | During a drag, `handleTouchMoved` (`0xebb0`) accepts an unblocked dock that matches some cargo when the touch is less than 40 units from a point 10 units ahead of the dock and the boat is more than 40 units away. `handlePathEndsAtDock` (`0x47fc`) trims the path tail, curves into a point 2, 5 or 10 units ahead of the dock according to boat size, and adds the dock itself. Manual targeting checks blocked status, not occupancy; `handleAutoDocking` (`0x8fa4`) separately rejects occupied and blocked docks. Unloading scores one point per cargo item, with a two-second base divided by the dock speed factor. | Matching drawn routes with a clear approach snap immediately, turn blue, show the dock flash and follow the reconstructed curved approach. A manual route can target an occupied dock and collide with its occupant; auto-docking avoids it. Treasure Island has no blocking monsters. All six imported docks and three boat sizes are tested, but the original was not run side by side and edge cases may differ. |
| Pacing and arrivals | `handlePacing` (`0xe708`) and `spawnBoat` (`0xd4d8`) manage stress, boat sizes, timing, spawn positions and obstruction checks. Pacing also controls the number of cargo colours and whether each cargo slot is selected independently. The original selects uniformly among available boat types and checks randomized entries at all four screen edges. The initial heading points toward the map center, the shoreline check covers the first 30 units inside the edge, and boats start 10, 15 or 25 units outside it. `cBoat::simulate` holds an arrival for two seconds while `cGameController::draw` shows a 37 × 27 arrow from the atlas, offset ahead of it by 30, 35 or 45 units with a five-unit oscillation. | The level settings, mixed-cargo stages, available-type selection, four-edge spawn and pressure-state model are integrated and covered by synthetic and HD-level tests. The arrival arrow and delay use those measurements; the oscillation is suppressed for reduced motion and an arrival in progress survives a saved shift. Warning detection, obstruction checks and exact probability distributions remain approximations. |
| Music and effects | The IPA contains a 54-second MP3 and CAF effects; the audio subsystem and preferences are named in the binary. | Audio is imported locally and played; separate music and effects volume settings persist on the device. Exact original mixing, timing, transitions and background behavior require additional comparison. |
| Fast-forward | The [gameplay video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BgPDMZNsuzk&t=107s) shows a fast-forward button. The native controller toggles it on release and multiplies simulation time by a preference whose default is 1.5×. | A toggle in the game bar applies the same default speed to the simulation, including boat movement and arrivals. It remains active when paused and returns to normal for a new or restored shift; the original's adjustable speed setting is not reproduced. |
| End-of-shift, records and rewind | Native `saveGame` (`0x9300`), `loadGame` (`0x1d314`), `setHighScore` (`0x14730`), `canRewind` (`0x854c`) and `rewind` (`0x8d58`) exist. Rewind checks and consumes a player's available rewind, removes crashed/warning/under-attack boats and some hazard objects; it should not be assumed to be a generic time-travel snapshot. | Local records and resuming in-progress shifts are implemented with browser-local state. Original profile/achievement menus and rewind are not implemented. |
| Non-Treasure Island modes | The binary contains classes and methods for cannons, pirates, cyclones, monsters, additional maps, and Bluetooth multiplayer. | They are not covered by this single-map implementation; absent HD assets and unavailable services limit verification. |

## Verification and remaining work

Current automated regression tests exercise movement, routing, land and screen-edge behavior, scoring, collision, docking and multitouch. Independent tests used the user's actual map geometry locally to check all six dock approaches and departures; original assets were not added to the repository. Tests prove those scenarios against our implementation, not semantic equivalence to the original app.

Priorities for a closer recreation are: validate path interpolation and turning against recorded original gameplay; compare pressure pacing, warning detection and docking to the original; and test audio, controls, restoration and visual timing on the target Android device. A broader project could implement original rewind semantics, then investigate additional locally supplied HD map assets if available.
